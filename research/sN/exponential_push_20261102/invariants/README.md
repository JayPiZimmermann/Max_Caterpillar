# Crease-cube invariants: exact span obstruction and limits

The live target remains the actual-network obstruction `H(k,3)`:
`GrowingMatching.LocalMatchingObstruction k 3`. No proof of this target,
nor a new unconditional width lower bound, was obtained here.

Two useful conclusions were obtained instead.

1. **The proposed unitwise forest-to-anchor reduction is false for every
   positive cover size.** A full-support conormal crease invariant gives
   an elementary all-real proof below. Independently parameterized
   examples were also found by the topology worker in the sibling
   polyhedral investigation. This is a mathematical proof, with exact
   finite controls, **not a Lean-verified span theorem**.
2. Within a fixed mixed projection of a disjoint adjacent-swap cube, all
   cancellation relations of the tested actual quadratic families are
   generated by four-unit rectangles. The underlying tensor identity
   has an elementary general proof. The rectangle identity itself is
   **Lean verified**, with only the standard three axioms. A concrete
   seven-input negative control shows why one cube cannot replace global
   chamber compatibility.

Only files in this isolated research directory were changed. Frozen
production sources, their assertions, and their receipts were untouched.

## Precise unitwise reduction refuted

For a quadratic set function

    G(B) = sum_{i in B} beta_i + sum_{{i,j} subset B} delta_ij,
    G(empty)=G(full)=0,

write `g=L_G` for its actual Lovasz/braid preactivation. All coefficients
are arbitrary real numbers. An anchor `A` means `G(B)=0` for every
`B` containing `A`. Let `C_r` consist of anchor-size-at-most-r units and
units whose interaction graph has vertex cover at most `r-1`.

The false claim is:

> Every `relu(g)` with forest interaction graph of vertex cover `r` is a
> finite signed linear combination of `C_r` units plus a shallow braid
> function.

The proof permits arbitrary signed readout coefficients, arbitrary
modular inner skips, all possible anchors and covers, and every shallow
braid remainder. It refutes the unitwise statement. It does **not**
refute a reduction of an entire already-glued exact network that uses
cancellation between different original units.

## Full-support conormal cube charge

Fix `r>=1`. Work on `n=2r+2` coordinates, partitioned into pairs
`(a_i,b_i)=(2i,2i+1)`, an extra coordinate `e=2r`, and `z=2r+1`.
Consider the `2^r` braid chambers that order pair blocks in their fixed
order, allow each pair to be flipped, then place `e`, then the common
minimum `z`. Thus swaps are adjacent and disjoint.

Let the full-support zero-sum normal be

    v_{a_i}=v_{b_i}=1,  v_e=-(2r+1),  v_z=1.

The hyperplane `H_v` meets the interior of every cube chamber. Its
proper-prefix sums are `1,2,...,2r,-1`, independent of the flips.
An explicit interior point has `x_z=0`, `x_e=t=r(2r+1)`, and values
`t+2r,...,t+1` on the ordered pair vertices. Its `v` dot product is zero.

For a continuous homogeneous piecewise-linear function `F`, define
`J_v(F,C)` to be its scalar crease coefficient on the portion of `H_v`
inside the open chamber `C`. For finite ReLU sums this coefficient is
unambiguous: on each open braid chamber each preactivation is linear;
distinct projective hyperplanes have independent distributional Hessian
measures away from their intersections. Normalize by `v_z=1`.
For `relu(u dot x)`, its contribution is `|lambda|` if
`u=lambda*v`, `lambda!=0`, and zero otherwise. Signed readout coefficients
multiply this number. A shallow braid function is affine on `C`, so its
contribution is zero.

Consequently `J_v` is linear on actual functions, not just on chosen
neuron parameterizations. Intersections with finitely many other
hyperplanes cause no problem: choose a relatively open subset of the
facet avoiding those intersections. Equivalently use its distributional
Hessian coefficient on that open facet.

Define the cube charge

    D_v(F) = sum_{eps in {0,1}^r} (-1)^|eps| J_v(F,C_eps).

### Why every C_r unit has zero charge

Swapping adjacent labels `a,b` changes the gradient of a quadratic
Lovasz extension by `delta_ab*(e_a-e_b)` up to the fixed sign convention.
In particular, when `delta_ab=0` the actual entire gradient is unchanged.
Thus any unit missing one selected pair has identical `J_v` on opposite
ends of that cube axis, and has zero charge.

If its graph has cover size at most `r-1`, one of the `r` disjoint pairs
misses the cover, hence is not an interaction edge.

If it is anchored at `A`, `|A|<=r`, there are two cases.

* If `z` is not in `A`, the penultimate prefix contains `A`, so its `G`
  value is zero. Since `G(full)=0`, the gradient at the last coordinate
  `z` is zero in every cube chamber. It cannot be proportional to the
  full-support `v`.
* If `z` is in `A`, only `r-1` anchor vertices remain to meet the `r`
  disjoint pairs. Pick a pair `{a,b}` disjoint from `A`. Anchoring and
  quadraticity give

      delta_ab = G(A+a+b)-G(A+a)-G(A+b)+G(A) = 0.

  It therefore misses one cube axis, as required.

These arguments allow arbitrary real coefficients and zero/singular
strata. They impose no genericity or positivity assumption on the unit.

### Forest unit with charge one

Take the actual normalized braid preactivation

    g(x) = v dot x + sum_i relu(x_{b_i}-x_{a_i}).

Its Boolean quadratic data are `delta_{a_i,b_i}=-1`; the singleton
coefficient at `b_i` is `v_{b_i}+1`, and all other singleton coefficients
are those of `v`. The interaction graph is exactly the `r` disjoint
edges. It is a forest with matching and vertex-cover number `r`.

In the unflipped chamber its gradient is exactly `v`. In any other cube
chamber the gradient is

    v + sum_{i flipped}(e_{b_i}-e_{a_i}).

Its last coefficient is still one, so it is proportional to `v` only
when no pair is flipped. Hence

    (J_v(relu(g),C_eps))_eps = (1,0,...,0),
    D_v(relu(g)) = 1.

Every proposed summand and every shallow remainder has charge zero.
This contradicts any proposed finite signed decomposition and proves
the claimed counterexample for every `r>=1`.

Already `r=1,n=4` suffices:

    v=(1,1,-3,1), g=v dot x + relu(x_1-x_0).

The two explicit hyperplane points are `(5,4,3,0)` and `(4,5,3,0)`;
the original `g` values are zero and one. A one-anchor unit can supply
this full-support normal in these chambers only when anchored at their
common minimum, coordinate three. It then supplies equal masses.

**Smallest possible repair:** an anchor supplying a nonzero r-cube
charge here must contain `z` and at least one endpoint of every selected
pair. Thus it needs at least `r+1` vertices. This argument does not
refute an anchor-`r+1` span reduction. It also does not establish one.

### Strengthening: full-rank spanning-tree counterexamples

The preceding counterexample is not dependent on low-dimensional ridge
terms. Add to `g` the comparison functions

    sum_{i=0}^{r-2} relu(x_{a_(i+1)}-x_{a_i})
      + relu(x_e-x_{a_0}) + relu(x_z-x_{a_0}).

Every added term vanishes on every chosen cube chamber. The crease pattern
and charge therefore remain unchanged. Their edges, together with the
selected pairs, form a **spanning tree** with `n-1` edges. The centers
`{a_0,...,a_(r-1)}` cover it, and the original `r` disjoint edges give the
opposite matching lower bound. Its cover and matching number are exactly
`r`. The comparison rows themselves span the full translation quotient:
their incidence matrix after deleting column `z` has determinant `+1` or
`-1`. All comparison coefficients in the preactivation are positive one.

For `r=1`, this is the full-rank star

    g=(1,1,-3,1) dot x
       +relu(x_1-x_0)+relu(x_2-x_0)+relu(x_3-x_0).

For `r=2` it is a full-rank double-star. Thus restricting the proposed
unitwise reduction to full-rank forest units does not repair it. A
network-wide cancellation-sensitive reduction is still not refuted.

## Fixed mixed projection: complete rectangle reduction

For `r` pair blocks and one final anchor, fix all unchanged chain
coordinates `eta_i=G(prefix_(2i))`. Write the two possible intermediate
values as

    G(prefix_(2i-2)+a_i) = (eta_(i-1)+eta_i)/2 + a_i',
    G(prefix_(2i-2)+b_i) = (eta_(i-1)+eta_i)/2 + b_i'.

Then `delta_{a_i,b_i}=-(a_i'+b_i')`. Every assignment is realized by an
actual quadratic set function supported on the disjoint pair edges:

    beta_{a_i}=(eta_i-eta_(i-1))/2+a_i',
    beta_{b_i}=(eta_i-eta_(i-1))/2+b_i',
    beta_anchor=-eta_r.

If some `a_i'+b_i'=0`, its matching number is at most `r-1`. When `eta`
itself has both signs, every full chain is mixed. Equality of crease
hyperplanes then means equality of every selected intermediate value;
the unchanged nonzero projection fixes the scale. The complete cube
equations are all transversal marginals of the endpoint coefficient table.

For a symmetric finite endpoint set of size `m`, let `V` be the free
vector space on endpoint pairs `(a,b)`, and

    T(e_(a,b))=e_a(old)+e_b(new).

Its kernel `K` is spanned by rectangles and has dimension `(m-1)^2`;
`rank(T)=2m-1`. Let `D` be the span of modular pairs `(a,-a)`, dimension
`m`. The restriction of `T` to `D` is injective. Choose a complement
`V=D direct-sum K direct-sum E`. This is possible because `D intersect K=0`.

The allowed coefficient tables span exactly the tensor words containing
at least one `D` factor. On tensor words with no `K` factor, `T^tensor r`
is injective. Thus its kernel in the allowed space is exactly the span
of words containing **both** a `D` factor and a `K` factor. Expanding the
`K` factor in rectangles shows that every cancellation is a sum of
single-axis rectangles with a distinct modular pair held fixed. These
rectangles remain in the allowed matching class.

Its dimensions are

    allowed dimension = m^(2r) - (m^2-m)^r,
    image dimension   = (2m-1)^r - (m-1)^r,
    kernel dimension  = allowed dimension - image dimension.

This applies to all finite signed real tables: enlarge any finite
endpoint set to a symmetric one. It is an elementary tensor proof,
not currently a Lean theorem. It establishes no obstruction by itself.

Each rectangular relation is a genuine global identity. For arbitrary
real `g,a,a',b,b',t`,

    sigma(g+a sigma(t)+b sigma(-t))
     -sigma(g+a' sigma(t)+b sigma(-t))
     -sigma(g+a sigma(t)+b' sigma(-t))
     +sigma(g+a' sigma(t)+b' sigma(-t)) = 0.

When `t>=0` the `b` terms vanish and cancel; when `t<=0` the `a` terms
vanish and cancel. `Rectangle.lean` proves this for arbitrary real
arguments and arbitrary shared nonlinear `g(x)`.

## Exact controls and semantic boundary

Run:

    python3 research/sN/exponential_push_20261102/invariants/cube_transport.py
    python3 research/sN/exponential_push_20261102/invariants/forest_anchor_counterexample.py
    lake env lean -j1 research/sN/exponential_push_20261102/invariants/Rectangle.lean

The cube script covers all endpoint values `{-1,0,1}`, all `r=1,2,3`
allowed tables and all `2^r` chambers. It recomputes actual Boolean
prefixes independently. For `r=2`, its 45 columns have kernel dimension
24, all generated by legal rectangles. For `r=3`, its 513 columns have
kernel dimension 396, all generated by legal rectangles. Modular rank
certificates establish rational lower bounds; the proved tensor
dimensions establish matching upper bounds. The one-factor ranks are
independently checked over rational arithmetic. There are 29,646 literal
Boolean prefix checks, with no noise floor or conditioning cutoff.

The forest script checks the explicit family for `r=1,...,5`, all cube
chambers, exact interior hyperplane points, and independently generated
primitive anchored/cover examples for `r<=3`. Its control examples include
genuine nonzero all-chamber crease mass; annihilation is not an artifact
of testing only units with no matching conormal. The script uses the
strengthened full-rank spanning-tree family, and checks its deleted-column
incidence determinant exactly over rational arithmetic.

The cube script also records an actual seven-input matching-two
preactivation with

    beta=(1,1,-2,-1,-2,2,-4), delta_01=2, delta_23=3.

All its chain entries are strictly positive throughout the complete
three-swap block cube, so the outer ReLU is affine on every one of those
eight chambers. Yet its full Boolean Mobius coefficient is two. At the
off-cube strictly ordered point `(23,21,19,17,15,0,13)` its preactivation
is zero and the outer function has a genuine interior crease; varying
coordinate six gives derivative jump magnitude four. This refutes using
one cube plus the missing-pair condition as a global Mobius annihilator.

## What remains viable

The conormal cube charge is linear on functions and kills the target MAX
as well: MAX has no interior braid-chamber creases. Thus it exposes
necessary cancellation equations, not a direct MAX obstruction.

A cancellation-sensitive replacement remains possible:

> Given a whole finite signed forest-unit family whose interior creases
> cancel on every braid chamber, construct one compatible lower-cover or
> anchored resolution modulo shallow braid functions.

Locally, vanishing top cube charge means that the crease field has no
top orientation Fourier coefficient, and can be written as a sum of
fields each missing one cube axis. The substantive missing step is to
realize those fields by actual lower-cover/anchored preactivations
**simultaneously across overlapping cubes, conormal classes, and zero
strata**. Treating this gluing as automatic repeats the failed unitwise
argument. A useful next finite test is a relative-span calculation on
the already available exact all-chamber forest library: determine
whether its entire crease kernel maps into the lower-class function
span, retaining a positive MAX6 control. No such result is asserted here.
