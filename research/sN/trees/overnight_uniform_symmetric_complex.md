# A top symmetric graph class, and the missing function map

25 October 2026. Uniform algebraic lemma, with exact finite sign controls.
This is not a separator for tree functions and proves no new width bound.

The exterior graph complex used in the upper search is acyclic in the
relevant degrees. Replacing the exterior product of edge generators by a
symmetric product retains a simple integral top class. Its proof needs no
homological machinery. The unresolved point is constructing a compatible
linear map from actual functions or opposite-face flags into this complex.

## Conventions and top cokernel

Let C_k^q be the free abelian group on multigraphs with k edges on the ordered
vertices 0,...,q-1, no loops, and no isolated vertices. Edges are unordered;
their generators commute. The normalized differential splits a vertex i
into adjacent vertices i,i+1, distributes its incident edges over both
vertices, and multiplies by (-1)^(i+1). Keep only terms in which both new
vertices occur. There is no sign from sorting the edge generators. These
are the symmetric conventions of the parent's normalized gap complex.

For every k>=1, the top cokernel

    C_k^(2k) / image(C_k^(2k-1) -> C_k^(2k))

is isomorphic to Z. A generator is the matching (01)(23)..., and the
isomorphism sends a perfect matching to (-1) to the number of its crossing
pairs in the given vertex order.

Proof. A graph in degree 2k has degree one at every vertex, hence is a
perfect matching. A graph in degree 2k-1 has exactly one degree-two vertex
and all other vertices degree one. Thus it consists of a cherry and k-2
disjoint edges; in particular no repeated edge is possible. Only the
degree-two vertex can split nontrivially. Its two incident edges must go
to different twins, giving two matchings with the same coefficient. The
two matchings differ by swapping the adjacent twins, which are not paired
to each other.

Conversely, merging any two adjacent unpaired vertices of a perfect
matching gives exactly such a source graph. Thus the relations are exactly

    M + M' = 0

for adjacent swaps of unpaired vertices. The crossing parity changes under
each such swap: equivalently, write each edge as the two-form b_u wedge b_v
with u<v, and wedge all edge forms. Swapping the two unpaired adjacent
coordinates changes the ambient volume sign and reverses no individual
edge. Therefore crossing parity annihilates every relation.

Every matching is connected to the standard matching by these swaps. Move
the partner of vertex 0 leftward until it reaches vertex 1; none of these
swaps exchanges paired vertices. Repeat on the remaining vertices. Hence
the quotient is generated by the standard matching. The parity functional
sends that generator to 1, proving that the quotient is Z, with no torsion.
For k=1 the source is empty and the same statement is immediate.

The elementary exterior-product realization is

    Sym^k(Lambda^2 V) -> Lambda^(2k) V,
    (b_a wedge b_b)...(b_c wedge b_d)
        |-> b_a wedge b_b wedge ... wedge b_c wedge b_d.

It is symmetric in the edge factors because each has even degree. Every
graph with repeated vertices maps to zero. This explains the surviving
perfect-matching character but does not connect it to a tree function.

## Exact controls

Run:

    python3 research/sN/trees/overnight_uniform_symmetric_complex.py

The checker uses only the standard library. It enumerates all perfect
matchings for k=1,...,5, compares crossing parity with independent inversion
parity, constructs every cherry source by adjacent merging, verifies its
two equal-sign boundary terms, and checks connectivity of the swap graph.
The source count is checked against the independent formula

    (2k-1) binom(2k-2,2) (2k-5)!!,

with the empty matching convention at k=2. Thus no matrix rank or modular
inference is involved. These finite checks support the uniform proof above.

## Exact residual for the lower-bound search

The graph class transforms by the sign character under permutation of its
2k vertices. A scalar symmetrization of this character kills a symmetric
maximum. Any successful construction must retain a second alternating
orientation factor, or an equivalent pairing-indexed tensor, before
forming a scalar. Squaring a score assigned to a tree does not suffice:
it generally destroys linearity in the underlying function.

The necessary missing construction is a linear, filtration-compatible map
from actual CPWL functions or their complete opposite-face data to the
graph complex (possibly tensored with its orientation line). Its behavior
on all root and apex placements must be proved, and lower-dimensional
functions must map to boundaries. The full-simplex class must map
nontrivially. None of those bridges follows from the algebraic lemma.

For the all-tree counting objective, the map need only annihilate canonical
units having two nonroot outward leaves at one parent, at every sufficiently
large odd arity. The canonical top-germ and leaf-pair counting argument in
all_tree_review.md already shows that this weaker analytic target gives an
exponential bound with rate (4/3)^(1/5). It is unnecessary to require a
separator of every tree without a perfect matching.

Two mandatory negative controls remain: a facet maximum belongs to the
lower filtration and must map to a boundary, and the exact twelve-term
whole-function relation from overnight_uniform_fay_function_verify.py must
receive zero. The previously refuted scalar chart averages and fixed-block
flag transfers do not provide this map.
