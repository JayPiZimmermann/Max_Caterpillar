# The finite Eulerian operator: an unmarked obstruction and a marked identity

This concerns the finite convolution logarithm in a connected graded Hopf
algebra. It is distinct from the completed edgewise logarithm in
`overnight_upper_logarithmic_states.md`.
On degree $N$,

\[
 e=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}k
       (\operatorname{id}-u\epsilon)^{\star k}.
\]

The sum is finite by conilpotence. The subalgebra generated by primitive
elements is cocommutative, so the usual Eulerian primitive projection
applies there; no such assertion is needed for an arbitrary element of
the full noncocommutative Hopf algebra.

## The current symmetric word element is killed

For a primitive singleton $p$,

\[
 e(p^N)=0\qquad(N>1).
\]

Indeed $\exp(tp)$ is group-like, so
$e(\exp(tp))=\log(\exp(tp))=tp$.
In FQSym, and hence in its Baxter subalgebra, $p^N$ is the sum of all
degree-$N$ permutations under shifted shuffle. Thus applying $e$
directly to the symmetric word sum used in an averaged construction
annihilates it. Exponential Baxter basis size cannot by itself turn
that operation into a nonzero simplex identity.

## One marked primitive survives

Let $a$ be another primitive, recording a head/apex mark. Define $B_n^+$
by

\[
 \frac z{1-e^{-z}}=\sum_{n\ge0}B_n^+\frac{z^n}{n!}.
\]

Then the exact uniform identity is

\[
 e(p^n a)=B_n^+\,\operatorname{ad}_p^n(a)
 =B_n^+\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom nj\,p^j a p^{n-j}.
\]

To prove it, apply $e$ to the group-like
$\exp(tp)\exp(sa)$ and differentiate its ordinary logarithm in $s$
at zero. If $Z(s)=\log(\exp(tp)\exp(sa))$, differentiating the
exponential gives

\[
 a=\frac{1-e^{-\operatorname{ad}_{tp}}}
          {\operatorname{ad}_{tp}}\,Z'(0).
\]

Formal inversion and comparison of the coefficient of $t^n$ proves
the identity. Every fixed graded coefficient is finite.

The initial values are $B_0^+=1$, $B_1^+=1/2$, $B_2^+=1/6$,
$B_4^+=-1/30$, and $B_6^+=1/42$. Odd indices greater than one vanish.
Thus this marked route naturally produces nonzero states in odd total
arity, together with arity-dependent denominators. If it eventually
gives a uniform construction at every odd arity, repeating one input
at the next odd arity would also handle even arities.

## The missing geometric map is essential

The $n+1$ commutator positions are not automatically different network
states. If an evaluation treats the mark only as an apex and ignores
its position, their images coincide and the binomial sum vanishes.
More generally their symmetric projection vanishes under the current
permutation-equivariant apex-only evaluation.

A viable marked map must encode mark motion as an actual head-transfer
or relative-boundary operation, with the corresponding correction terms.
It must then intertwine the actual shifted-shuffle/Baxter product and
coproduct. Ordinary Minkowski product, cubical concatenation, and raw
products of independent BST kernels have not supplied this map.

This identity therefore gives a precise arity-dependent algebraic
candidate, not an exponential construction. A finite Baxter expansion
is only the algebraic support count; its geometric image and all
lower-dimensional network corrections must still be controlled.

The adjacent standard-library checker computes the convolution directly
by distributing $p^n a$ into nonempty ordered tensor slots. It agrees
with the independently computed reciprocal-series coefficients through
$n=12$, and checks the unmarked vanishing through degree 17.
