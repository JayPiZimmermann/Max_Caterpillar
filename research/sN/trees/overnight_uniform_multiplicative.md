# A faithful multiplicative deformation of canonical tree kernels

25 October 2026. Uniform mathematical construction. It respects all rational
kernel relations and every apex placement. It is not yet a separator of
outward twins and does not prove an unrestricted exponential lower bound.

Let N>=3, d=N-2, a be the apex frequency, and q_e the cut frequency away
from the root. Put epsilon_e=+1 when the edge function has its positive
endpoint away from the root, and epsilon_e=-1 otherwise. The generic kernel
is

    R_U = (-a)^(d-2) / product_e[q_e(q_e+epsilon_e a)].

Write A=z^a and Q_e=z^q_e for characters of the lattice Z^N/Z(1,...,1),
so the label characters satisfy product_i z_i=1. If n_- is the number of
negative epsilon_e, define

    G_U = (-1)^d A^(1-n_-) (product_e Q_e) (1-A)^(d-2)
          / product_e[(1-Q_e)(1-A^epsilon_e Q_e)].                 (1)

Then R_U -> G_U extends LINEARLY to the canonical rational-kernel span.
It preserves every linear relation, including those mixing apex labels,
and is injective on that span. The maximum is sent to

    G_max = (-1)^(N-1) / product_i(1-z_i).                         (2)

The sign is positive at the odd arities needed for the lower-bound target.
It is negative at N=2, where sigma(x) has generating function z/(1-z)^2.

## Global descent before any unit-adapted boundary choice

Use the common arrangement Delta and the marked simple-fraction embedding
from overnight_uniform_os_bridge.md. Every omega_j R belongs to S_Delta.
The half-open lattice construction in overnight_uniform_halfopen.md gives
a linear map H_eta on S_Delta respecting its complete circuit presentation.
General nonunimodular bases use their full fundamental-parallelepiped
numerator, not the single-monomial formula valid for canonical bases.

Extend H_eta to the differential module generated by S_Delta: a frequency
derivative partial_i acts on lattice series as minus the logarithmic
derivative D_i=z_i partial/partial z_i. This extension is well-defined
because the rational principal-part module is free over the derivative
ring with generators S_Delta. Equivalently, apply the same half-open lattice
evaluation to polynomially weighted cone indicators. This is the
cone/Laplace model of [Brion–Vergne's decomposition](https://arxiv.org/html/math/9903178).

For the kernels here the resulting image is independent of eta. Indeed,
the inverse polarized Laplace transform of R is a homogeneous piecewise
linear spline. Every coordinate multiple omega_i R has an inverse transform
which is an ordinary piecewise constant function, because it lies in
S_Delta. Thus the spline has no distributional facet jumps: otherwise a
coordinate derivative would contain a nonzero facet delta. Its linear
pieces consequently fit continuously across all facets. The values at
lattice points are independent of the common infinitesimal boundary shift.
The fixed global polarization is retained throughout this argument.

More explicitly, let F be this continuous degree-one spline and f_i its
bounded piecewise constant weak derivatives. At a lattice point m, Euler's
identity gives

    sum_i m_i f_i(m+epsilon eta)
      = F(m+epsilon eta)-epsilon sum_i eta_i f_i(m+epsilon eta)
      -> F(m).

The left side stabilizes for sufficiently small positive epsilon. This
justifies that logarithmic derivatives weight the lattice point m itself,
not the displaced point m+epsilon eta, and proves eta-independence directly.

This continuity argument is essential. Only AFTER establishing it may we
choose a convenient eta separately when calculating the same global image
of an individual unit. The proof does not silently combine unrelated
unit-dependent cone maps.

In any r=N-1 independent frequency coordinates x_i, homogeneity gives

    R = -sum_i partial_i(x_i R).

Therefore its global lattice image is

    G_R = sum_i D_i H_eta(x_i R).                                 (3)

All input functions in the lower-dimensional span already have zero generic
Fourier kernel. The construction is applied to that kernel, so no assertion
that irrational lower-dimensional functions have an integer period is used.

## Compute the image of one canonical unit

Choose the independent coordinates (a,q_1,...,q_d), and choose eta with all
its q-coordinates positive and its a-coordinate larger than their sum.
For every shifted basis (a,q_e+b_e epsilon_e a), all basis coordinates of
eta are positive. Hence every half-open numerator is one. The elementary
partial fractions then give, with G denoting the right side of (1),

    H_eta(aR)   = (A^(-1)-1) G,
    H_eta(q_jR) = A^(-1_{epsilon_j=+1})(Q_j^(-1)-1) G.             (4)

These are the unpolarized basis formulas. They equal the fixed globally
polarized formulas: reversing a ray complements its boundary convention,
and 1-z^(-beta)=-z^(-beta)(1-z^beta) supplies exactly the polarization sign.
Thus the convenient calculation changes neither the polarization nor the
global map.

For example, each difference in the aR expansion becomes

    1/(1-Q) - 1/(1-A^epsilon Q)
      = Q(1-A^epsilon)/[(1-Q)(1-A^epsilon Q)].

Using 1-A^(-1)=-A^(-1)(1-A) produces exactly (4), including its monomial
and sign factors.

Substitution in (3) gives G itself. One short check uses logarithmic
derivatives of (1). Combining the q_j derivative with its contribution to
the a derivative gives 1 for epsilon_j=+1 and A^(-1) for epsilon_j=-1:

    (1-Q)/(1-AQ) + (1-A)Q/(1-AQ) = 1,
    (1-Q)/(A-Q) - (1-A)Q/[A(A-Q)] = A^(-1).

The remaining scalar is

    -A^(-1)+(A^(-1)-1)(1-n_-)-(d-2)+(d-n_-)+n_-/A = 1.

This proves (1) through a single globally defined linear operation.

For (2), take a boundary vector whose label order is 0<1<...<N-1. The
marked basis fractions of the simplex have numerators equal to the product
of all preceding label characters. Their sum telescopes to zero because
product_i z_i=1. Applying (3) gives 1/product_i(1-z_i), multiplied by the
original maximum normalization (-1)^(N-1).

Finally set z_i=exp(-t omega_i), with sum_i omega_i=0. Then

    G_U(exp(-t omega)) = t^(-N) R_U(omega) + higher powers of t.

The same holds for the maximum. Thus a relation among the G_U implies the
original relation among the R_U. Together with global linear descent, this
proves faithfulness of the deformation.

## Independent finite-difference derivation and convergence convention

In the unit's integral coordinates put

    g(t,s)=sigma(t+sum_e sigma(s_e)).

For forward differences Delta f(k)=f(k+1)-f(k),

    Delta_t^2 product_e Delta_(s_e) g
      = (product_e 1_(s_e>=0)) Delta_t^(d+2) sigma(t+sum_e s_e).

Also Delta_t^2 sigma(k)=1_(k=-1). The rational generating-function normal
form is therefore

    (1-z_t^(-1))^(d-2) z_t^(-1)
      / product_e[(1-z_(s_e)^(-1))(1-z_(s_e)/z_t)].

Substitute z_t=A^(-1) and z_(s_e)=Q_e^epsilon_e to recover (1).

This computation is NOT a claim that the bilateral series of the globally
growing function converges. Formally, take the module of lattice sequences
under integer shifts and localize by the nonzero factors 1-shift_v. A
rational half-open cone indicator has a rational normal form: a finite
difference in each independent ray direction reduces it to its finite
fundamental-parallelepiped numerator. Polynomial cone weights are treated
by logarithmic derivatives. Rational polyhedral CPWL functions admit finite
pointed-cone decompositions, so this gives their rational normal form. The
delta sequence is not killed by a nonzero shift polynomial, since its
translates are linearly independent; hence this normal form is unambiguous.
The globally defined cone map above supplies the separate bridge from the
generic Fourier quotient to this lattice calculation.
