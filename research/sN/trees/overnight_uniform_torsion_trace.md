# All-character pole rules and the quadratic trace control

25 October 2026. Exact structural facts about the faithful multiplicative
model, followed by one failed scalar contraction. No separator is claimed.

## The matching condition at a positive-apex character

Let chi be a character with chi_i in {+1,-1} and product_i chi_i=1. Its
negative-label set B has even size. Approach it along z_i=chi_i exp(t h_i),
where sum_i h_i=0 and h is generic for every cut and shifted cut. Pole order
below is signed: a negative pole order means a zero.

If the actual apex has positive character, the cut character Q_e is negative
exactly when the component away from the root contains an odd number of
labels of B. Those edges are the unique B-join J_T(B): the subgraph whose
odd-degree vertex set is B. Existence follows by the cut-parity rule and
uniqueness follows because a forest has no nonempty even-degree subgraph.

Every edge outside this join gives two zero denominator factors. Every
edge in the join gives none. The numerator contributes a zero of order
N-4. Hence the exact generic pole order of G_U is

    N-2|J_T(B)|.

Every B-join has at least |B|/2 edges. Equality holds exactly when its edges
form a matching with endpoint set B: all B vertices must have degree one
and every other vertex degree zero. The target pole order is N-|B|.

Thus after the common normalization

    F_U(z) = [product_i(1-z_i)] G_U(z),

every positive-apex term is regular along the generic approach, and it can
have a nonzero constant term only if the B-join is a matching. This is an
exact matching rule for this apex sector, not an all-apex separator.

If the actual apex has negative character, precisely one denominator factor
per edge vanishes. The numerator is nonzero. Its G_U pole order is N-2,
so its normalized F_U pole order is |B|-2, independently of tree shape.
These wrong-apex contributions require higher expansion terms at every
|B|>=4. They cannot be dropped when forming a uniform constant coefficient.

## Uniform averaging is only dilation

Let E be the full group of 2-torsion characters, with |E|=2^(N-1). The
faithful cone-lattice model gives the exact distribution relation

    sum_(chi in E) G_R(chi z) = 2^N G_R(z^2).

Indeed, all series converge in a common polarized region. Character
orthogonality selects the even lattice points, and the continuous inverse
Laplace spline has degree one, so its value at 2m is twice its value at m.
Rational continuation gives the identity everywhere. Thus unweighted
character averaging alone does not distinguish tree families.

## One canonical quadratic weight

For odd N=2k+1, the function

    q(B)=|B|/2 modulo 2

is a quadratic form on the even-subset group. Its polar form is intersection
parity. This form is nondegenerate: the orthogonal complement of the
even-subset space is generated by the all-one vector, which is not itself
even when N is odd. Its Gauss sum is

    sum_B (-1)^q(B) = Re(1+i)^N = +/- 2^k.

This supplies a canonical parity weight without fitting coefficients.
Define the actual linear functional

    Lambda_h(R) = CT_(t=0) sum_(B even) (-1)^(|B|/2)
                    F_R(chi_B exp(t h)).

Every term uses the SAME constant-coefficient rule on the normalized
function, so terms of different pole orders are compared consistently.
The maximum has F_max=1 at odd arity and receives the nonzero Gauss sum.
The version omitting B=empty is also well-defined.

Before taking the scalar constant coefficient, the Gaussian trace is
invertible: its square is 2^(N-1) times the identity. This follows directly
from nondegeneracy and quadratic character orthogonality. Therefore no
claim is made that the uncontracted trace itself annihilates a tree.

## Exact five-label failure

Use h=(-40,1,3,9,27). For an outward star with apex a and center c,
the normalized multiplicative kernel simplifies to

    F_(a,c) = (1-z_c^(-1))(1-z_a)^2
               / product_(l!=a,c)(1-z_a z_l).

The full unaveraged label orbit is six times the sum over the twenty
ordered apex/center choices. Exact constant coefficients summed by negative
set size are

| Negative-set size | Full-star value summed over characters |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 7210557/208754 |
| 2 | 519060327/1356901 |
| 4 | 502268953/2713802 |

Consequently the full Gaussian trace gives

    outward star: -221057230/1356901,       maximum: -4.

Omitting the identity character gives

    outward star: -76550243/387686,         maximum: -5.

Both proposed scalar contractions therefore fail. The producer expands the
simplified star expression in exponential coordinates using exact rational
arithmetic. The independent checker imports no producer and starts instead
from the unsimplified G_U formula, multiplies all five normalization factors,
and expands in t-1. It converts that result using

    CT f(exp(tau)) = Res f(t) dt/[t log t].

Here all poles have order at most two, and reciprocity gives c_(-1)=c_(-2),
so the conversion is c_0-c_(-1)/2+5c_(-2)/12. Every higher pole coefficient
is checked to vanish before using that formula. The two routes agree exactly.
