# Exact obstruction to positive folding in the finite four-dimensional Hadamard orbit

This is a research-route exclusion, not a lower bound for arbitrary networks
or an answer to the unrestricted second hidden layer width question.

Let

\[
 q(x)=\max(0,\min(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)).
\]

Let \(\mathcal G\) be generated by signed coordinate permutations and the
normalized Sylvester Hadamard matrix \(H/2\). Its exact order is 1152 and its
orbit of \(q\) consists of 48 functions. They are the 16 sign choices in each
of three orthogonal frames: the coordinate frame and the two frames of
half-sign vectors with fixed sign parity. All their possible walls belong
to the following 24 hyperplanes:

\[
 x_i=0,\qquad x_i\pm x_j=0,\qquad
 x_1\pm x_2\pm x_3\pm x_4=0.
\]

The last list has eight members; the pair list has twelve.

**Theorem.** No nonzero nonnegative linear combination of these 48 functions
is a single outer ReLU of a finite shallow preactivation. The shallow
preactivation may have arbitrary real directions and coefficients; it need
not use the 24 displayed walls. Biases do not change the conclusion.

## Seven-point proof

Suppose

\[
 F=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal O} w_Q Q=\sigma(g),\qquad
 w_Q\ge0,\quad \sum_Qw_Q>0.
\]

The group acts transitively on the orbit and preserves the class of shallow
functions. After precomposition, assume the coefficient of \(q\) is positive.
Then \(F>0\) on the open positive orthant \(C\), so \(g=F\) on \(C\) and, by
continuity, on its closure.

Consolidate proportional first hidden layer walls of \(g\). A wall outside
the 24 displayed hyperplanes that intersects \(C\) has zero coefficient:
at a generic point of that intersection, \(F\) is linear, whereas a
nonzero consolidated shallow coefficient would give a nonzero gradient
jump. A wall that does not intersect \(C\) gives a linear function on
\(C\). Consequently, the restriction of \(g\) to \(\overline C\) lies in

\[
 \operatorname{span}\{x_i,\ |h(x)|:h\text{ is one of the 24 displayed normals}\}.
\]

Every function in this span satisfies the exact evaluation identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
 g(1,1,1,3)={}&-g(0,0,1,1)-g(0,1,1,0)+g(0,1,1,2)\\
 &-g(1,0,1,0)+g(1,0,1,2)+g(1,1,2,0).
\end{aligned}
\]

This is checked directly on the four coordinate functions and the 24
absolute values. Every orbit function vanishes at all six points on the
right. Therefore the right side is zero, since \(g=F\) there. But
\(q(1,1,1,3)=1\), so the left side is at least \(w_q>0\), a contradiction.

For a biased shallow preactivation, replace it by its homogeneous recession
function: \(g_\infty(x)=\lim_{t\to\infty}g(tx)/t\). Homogeneity of \(F\)
gives \(\sigma(g_\infty)=F\), reducing to the preceding proof.

## Independent exact checker

`overnight_fixed_f4_verify.py` uses only Python's standard library. It does
not read or import the numerical producer or its certificate. It builds
the three frames explicitly, verifies their orthogonality, constructs all
48 functions, and checks closure and transitivity under the specified
generators. It then reconstructs the 24 potential walls and verifies the
seven-point identity and all 288 boundary evaluations exactly. At the
interior point only two orbit functions are nonzero, each with value one.

The checker certifies the finite identities. The wall-elimination and
recession arguments above provide the bridge to arbitrary shallow
preactivations. No Lean formalization is claimed.

Run in this directory:

```sh
python3 overnight_fixed_f4_verify.py
```

## The invariant average control

The separate exact average check has four ray orbits. The invariant shallow
span on this arrangement has two generators: the sum of absolute values
of the normalized short roots and the sum for the long roots. Evaluations
are as follows, using one representative for each ray orbit.

| Ray | Short-root sum | Long-root sum | Sum of all 48 orbit functions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \((-3,-1,-1,-1)\) | 18 | 24 | 2 |
| \((-2,-1,-1,0)\) | 12 | 18 | 0 |
| \((-1,-1,-1,-1)\) | 10 | 12 | 2 |
| \((-1,-1,0,0)\) | 6 | 10 | 0 |

The two zero rows force both invariant shallow coefficients to vanish,
contradicting either positive row. Averaging therefore also excludes any
nonzero nonnegative shallow linear combination of orbit functions. The
seven-point theorem is stronger: it excludes a positive combination even
after a single outer ReLU.

## Discovery and scope

The exact group inventory has 1152 matrices, three frames, 48 functions,
24 walls, and 240 primitive oriented rays. A preliminary linear search of
32 stabilizer orbits of three-copy folds and a bounded mixed-integer search
of folds with up to six copies found no candidate. These unsuccessful
searches are diagnostic only. The subsequent unbounded linear relaxation
returned a seven-row rational contradiction, which led to the independent
checker and proof above. The theorem does not depend on either bounded
search.

This closes the proposed positive-fold mechanism for this particular
finite Hadamard orbit. It does not rule out signed fold identities,
additional transformed functions, or other constructions of maximum.
