\noindent\textbf{Proof status.}
The width theorem below is already verified in Lean for ordinary networks
with arbitrary biases. This draft replaces its normalization and probability
arguments by simpler proofs. It is not yet a self-contained replacement:
the existing finite proof of \Cref{lem:five-input-obstruction} has not yet
been replaced by a proof in this document. The new normalization is also
verified in Lean; the fixed-pair counting argument has an independent
mathematical audit and is awaiting its own Lean formalization.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:clean-quadratic-width}
For every $C>0$ there exists $c_C>0$ such that the following holds for
every $N\ge3$. If an ordinary two-hidden-layer ReLU network with arbitrary
real weights and biases represents $\operatorname{MAX}_N$ on all of
$\mathbb R^N$, and its hidden widths satisfy $h_1\le CN^2$, then
$h_2\ge c_C N^2$.
\end{theorem}

The ordinary architecture is specified in
\Cref{eq:clean-ordinary-network}; it has no skip connections.
The proof uses linear and shallow terms only in intermediate representations.
A separate formal theorem records an explicit numerical constant; its
optimization plays no role in this draft.

\section{The structural ingredient}
\begin{lemma}[Five-input obstruction]\label{lem:five-input-obstruction}
Suppose a normalized representation of $\operatorname{MAX}_5$ has the
form in \Cref{eq:clean-normalized-network}, and every nonlinear first-layer
function is a scalar multiple of $\sigma(x_i-x_j)$.
For each second-layer preactivation, combine coefficients and orient the
comparisons to write
\[
 g_k(x)=\ell_k(x)+\sum_{i<j}a_{k,ij}\sigma(x_i-x_j).
\]
Then at least one graph with edges $\{i,j\}$ for which $a_{k,ij}\ne0$
contains two disjoint edges.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Remaining proof obligation for this draft.}
The existing all-real proof uses a star-neuron certificate and a reduction
of graphs without two disjoint edges. Its coefficient identities, real
parameter coverage, and network interpretation are checked in Lean.
A new cycle-deletion proof removes the separate triangle treatment, but
retains the star certificate. The present simplification search aims to
replace that certificate by explicit identities and a short treatment of
their degenerate cases. The arguments below use the established lemma;
they do not prove it anew.
